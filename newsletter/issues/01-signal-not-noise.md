# Signal, not noise

*Welcome to **North de Noise** — institutional-grade research, no minimums, no noise.*

Here's the bet this newsletter is making: **99% of finance content is noise**, and
you already know it. It's built to be watched, not to be right — engineered for
outrage, FOMO, and the algorithm, with all the nutritional value of a thumbnail.
Whether you're actually better off a year from now is not, it turns out, the
business model.

I'm going to do the opposite, and be a little rude about the difference. I'm a
former financial advisor turned independent researcher — you can verify me on
[FINRA BrokerCheck (CRD 6510444)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/individual/summary/6510444),
which is more than the median guru can say — and I hold myself to the standard a
registered firm would, even writing independently. The jokes are free. The
standard underneath them is the entire product. Strategic irreverence, grounded
in virtue.

## The Read — the standard I hold myself to

Four rules. If a piece of finance content (including mine) breaks them, treat it
as noise:

1. **Fair and balanced.** Every return number comes with the risk that produced
   it, labeled as *past* performance. Past performance does not indicate or
   guarantee future results — the one sentence everyone quotes and nobody believes.
   Believe it. Any source that "forgets" to say it is selling you something.
2. **Substantiated, not promissory.** No guarantees. No "this will." Claims tie
   back to things you can check.
3. **Educational, not individualized advice.** I don't know your situation, so
   nothing here is a recommendation for *you*. It's a way of thinking, not a
   trade ticket.
4. **Conflicts disclosed.** If I have a position or a product in play, I say so,
   in the issue, where you'll see it.

Boring? Yes. That's the point. Boring is what survives a bad week.

## Signal vs. Noise — the genre of the guarantee

> **The Noise:** *"Guaranteed returns." "This can't lose." "Risk-free X%."*

There is no such thing as a guaranteed return above the risk-free rate, and even
"risk-free" only means *default*-free, not *loss*-free (ask anyone who held a
long bond into a rate hike). The moment someone guarantees an outcome in markets,
they've told you they either don't understand risk or are betting you don't.

> **The Signal:** probabilities, ranges, and "it depends." Real analysis sounds
> less confident than the noise — because it's honest about what it can't know.

## The Number — 1%

The share of finance content worth your attention, give or take. The job of this
newsletter is to find it for you so you don't have to wade through the other 99%.

## What I'd Watch

This week, just notice the **language** of the finance content you consume. Count
the guarantees, the screenshots-without-risk, the anonymous confidence. You'll
start to see the noise as noise. That's the first edge.
