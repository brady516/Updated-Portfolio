# LinkedIn playbook — North de Noise personal brand

*Personal / distribution asset — Brady's to optimize. Deliberately NOT part of the
sellable email-bridge venture.*

## The diagnosis (from your own Premium analytics)

- **Reach is dormant, not declining.** ~336 impressions / 90 days ≈ 3–4/day. You're
  barely surfacing — because you barely post.
- **The people who land, read.** 1m 30s avg view time is *good* — you have an interest
  problem's opposite: engaged readers, too few of them.
- **68% of clicks hit your Intro**, ~19% Activity, ~13% Experience. Visitors check
  *who you are*, then leave — because there's no Activity feeding them.
- **Recruiters are viewing you** → your profile still reads *"for hire,"* not *founder
  of a research brand.*

**One-line takeaway:** a warm, high-intent profile that you're feeding nothing. Fix the
static foundation (Intro), then turn on the content engine (Activity). LinkedIn is
showing you an open door.

---

## 1. Foundation — fix the static profile first (highest leverage)

Because 68% of clicks land on the Intro, this is where an hour returns the most.

### Headline (the line that shows everywhere)
Reposition from candidate → practitioner-with-a-brand. Lead with what you do *for the
reader* + verifiable proof. Draft:

> **Licensed analyst turning institutional-grade research into plain English · Founder,
> North de Noise · MBA, Series 7/63/66 (CRD 6510444)**

### About / summary (rewrite — paste-ready starting point)
Lead with the manifesto hook, prove the standard, tell the origin that earns trust,
close with a CTA to the newsletter.

> **99% of finance content is noise.** Guaranteed-return promises, anonymous gurus,
> "sophisticated" products that are table stakes with a markup. I write the other 1% —
> institutional-grade research, in plain English, with the risk left in.
>
> I spent 13 years as a trading amateur before I did it properly: an MBA in
> investments, then Series 7, 63, and 66, and a seat as a 3(21) fiduciary managing
> retirement money for savers. My record is public — FINRA BrokerCheck, CRD 6510444.
> That's the whole point: you can check me, and you can't check the noise.
>
> Every week in **North de Noise**, I take one piece of bad finance advice apart and
> rebuild it with the math the original left out — plus one bit of Wall Street jargon
> translated to what it's actually hiding.
>
> Educational, never individualized advice. If a claim can't be verified, it doesn't
> run. → **[newsletter link]**

### Featured section
Pin three: (1) the newsletter subscribe page, (2) your most shareable teardown
(the four-square / issue #13), (3) northdenoise.com.

### Recruiter setting
Decide consciously: if you're building the brand, turn **"Open to work" off** — the
recruiter views are a signal your profile still markets you as *available talent*,
which undercuts *"founder with a research desk."* (Leave it on only if you actively
want the inbound.)

## 2. The content engine — turn on Activity (the real lever)

Your Activity bar is flat because there's no activity. This is the fix, and it plugs
straight into the newsletter you're already writing.

### Cadence (realistic, sustainable > heroic)
**2–3 posts/week.** Consistency beats volume. One dropped week resets the algorithm's
sense of you.

### The repurpose loop (near-zero extra work)
Every North de Noise issue already contains the posts. From one issue:
- **Signal vs. Noise teardown** → one native post (your highest-shareability unit).
- **Wall Street Word Salad** → one short punchy post (jargon → plain English).
- **The Number** → a one-chart/one-stat post.

The newsletter is the factory; LinkedIn is the storefront window.

### Content pillars (rotate)
1. **Teardowns** — "here's the bad take, here's the honest rebuild." The viral unit.
2. **Word Salad** — decode one piece of jargon. Fast, funny, shareable.
3. **Origin/scar stories** — the four-square, the 3(21)→founder wall. True, personal,
   trust-building. These outperform everything.
4. **Plain-English explainers** — one concept the noise overcomplicates.

### Format rules (how LinkedIn actually rewards you)
- **Hook in line one.** It's all most people see before "…more." Make it a stop.
- **Short lines, white space.** Thumb-readable.
- **One idea per post.** Don't bundle.
- **Link in the FIRST COMMENT, not the body** — LinkedIn throttles outbound links in
  posts. Say "link in comments."
- **End with a question** — comments are the algorithm's oxygen.
- **Native text/carousels > link posts.** Keep people on-platform.

---

## 3. Distribution & engagement

- **Reply to every comment**, fast. Early engagement compounds reach — and it's where
  relationships (and subscribers) actually form.
- **Warm up before you post:** spend 10 min commenting thoughtfully on others' posts in
  your niche; it primes your reach.
- **Post, then engage for 30–60 min** — the first hour's engagement decides distribution.
- **DM new followers/commenters** a genuine thanks + the newsletter link. Not spammy —
  human.
- **Comment on bigger finance accounts** with real substance; borrowed audience is the
  fastest organic reach there is.

Watch the same panel, but the *right* numbers (ignore noisy weekly %s on small bases):

| Metric | Now | 30-day goal | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions / 90d | ~336 | trending up weekly | reach waking up |

| Profile → newsletter signups | ? | track it | the whole point |

**The flip:** Week 1 — rewrite headline + About + Featured, turn recruiter signal off.
Weeks 1–4 — post 2–3×/week from the newsletter repurpose loop, reply to every comment.
By day 30 impressions climb, Activity share rises, and the profile becomes a funnel
instead of a résumé.

## Integrity (same bar as everything)

Real credentials only, results labeled historical, educational-not-advice, no
guarantees, conflicts disclosed. On a public professional surface the discipline
matters *more*, not less — it's the exact thing the noise can't fake, and your entire
edge. Describe licenses accurately (held; not currently registered) — BrokerCheck is
one click away, so precision is non-negotiable.
