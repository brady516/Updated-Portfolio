# Conversion psychology — the honest version

Six UX/behavioral principles that decide whether a page converts. **Every one is a
lever the Scourge uses to manipulate — and used honestly, each is just clear
communication that respects how people actually decide.** Your brand's promise is
*don't get taken advantage of,* so you may only ever use the **honest half** of each.
The firewall is the brand, not a tax on it. (Same discipline as the growth-engine
"steal the mechanics, refuse the hype.")

Applies to every conversion surface: the North de Noise site + store + Substack signup,
the LookLegit landing + package pages + intake forms + phone close, and the RIA
proforma.

---

### 1. Smart defaults — pre-select the most common choice
People read a default as a *recommendation*, and 70–90% never change it.

- **Take (honest):** pre-select the choice that's genuinely best/most common. On the
  LookLegit intake, default the domain question sensibly; on the package selector,
  default to the tier you'd honestly recommend most people. Turn a blank form into
  "scan and adjust," not "decide from scratch."
- **Refuse (dark):** defaulting people into the *most profitable* option, or
  pre-checking add-ons they didn't ask for. That's the exact "taken advantage of" you
  exist to kill. Default to the *right* choice, never the priciest.

### 2. Goal gradient — never start a user at zero
The closer people feel to done, the faster they finish. You choose where the start line
sits.

- **Take (honest):** show real progress. A multi-step intake as "Step 1 of 3 ✓" the
  moment they've entered their email — because they *did* start. The segmentation quiz
  as "you're 25% of the way to your setup."
- **Refuse (dark):** a *fabricated* progress bar that misrepresents effort left. Count a
  step they actually took; never invent a "20%" that means nothing. Momentum from truth,
  not theater.

### 3. Reciprocity — give real value first  ★ your core strength
Cialdini's #1 driver. A genuine gift creates a pull to reciprocate; free samples lift
purchases enormously. **You already run this** — the free Valuation Lab, free newsletter
issues before the paywall, the store's "prove quality free first."

- **Take (honest):** give something genuinely useful *before* asking. For LookLegit: a
  **free 3-point "does your business look legit online?" mini-audit** — tell them the
  real issues (gmail address, no Google Business Profile, no reviews), free, then offer
  the fix. Real value delivered = trust + demonstrated expertise + open door.
- **Refuse (dark):** the *hostage* version — run their scan, then blur the results
  behind "create an account to see." That's holding value ransom; it reads as a con and
  it's the opposite of your brand. Give the real partial report, then invite.

### 4. Endowment / IKEA effect — let them own it before they pay  ★ already yours
People value what they build or feel they own. Your **"everything in your name, you own
it all"** is the endowment effect made *real and honest* — not a feeling, a fact.

- **Take (honest):** let them make real choices early — pick their email address
  (`name@business.com`), see a preview of their site — so it feels like *theirs* before
  checkout, because it will *be* theirs.
- **Refuse (dark):** manufacture the feeling of ownership and then lock them in / hold
  the domain in your name / trap them in a retainer. You already refuse this by design —
  it's your whole differentiator against agencies.

### 5. Loss aversion — show the real loss, not a fake threat  ★ your hero does this
Losing hurts ~2x more than gaining feels good. **Your landing hero already uses it
honestly:** *"your email is losing you bids before you pick up the phone"* — a true cost.

- **Take (honest):** frame *genuine* losses they're already incurring — jobs lost to a
  more legit-looking competitor, quotes dying in voicemail. True, so it's fair.
- **Refuse (dark):** manufactured threats and fake countdowns (the video's "I'll risk
  it" + timer on your files). Fabricated urgency is the Scourge's move and it's already
  on your refuse list. Real stakes, never invented ones.

### 6. Contrast / anchoring — set the ruler with a *real* number  ★ already yours
The brain judges every price relative to the one it saw just before. **You already
anchor honestly** — "$90/month SaaS vs. $299 once," "an advisory build runs four
figures," and for the RIA tool, "eMoney is $3,600/yr for one feature."

- **Take (honest):** lead with a *true* comparable cost so your price reads as the
  rounding error it is. Show the package ladder high-to-low so each tier frames the next.
- **Refuse (dark):** fake strikethrough anchors ("~~$999~~ $299") for a price that was
  never real, or decoy tiers designed to mislead. Anchor against *real* reference
  prices only.

---

## Run this against any page you build

- [ ] Is every default the choice I'd *honestly recommend* — not the most profitable?
- [ ] Does the user get **real value before** I ask for anything? (reciprocity)
- [ ] Does the flow show **true** progress, never fake?
- [ ] Can they **build/choose** something that becomes genuinely theirs?
- [ ] Is any urgency or loss I show **real**, verifiable, un-manufactured?
- [ ] Is every price anchored to a **true** comparable, no fake strikethroughs?
- [ ] Would I be comfortable if the customer saw *exactly why* each element works?

That last box is the whole test. If the mechanism only works because the user *doesn't*
see it, it's a dark pattern — cut it. If it works even when they understand it, it's
just good, honest design. Your brand can only ever ship the second kind.
