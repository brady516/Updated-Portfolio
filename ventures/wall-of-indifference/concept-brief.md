# Concept brief — a builder-native planning engine for the advisors who chase founders

**Working codename:** *Trajectory* (alt names: *Liftoff*, *North Star Model*, *The
Builder's Monte Carlo*). The product name is TBD; **"the Wall of Indifference"** is
the marketing hook / named enemy, not the product name.

**Status:** pre-build concept. Nothing is committed to code. This brief exists to
**test demand before building** (per the validate-first loop) and to put in front
of a real RIA or attorney to see if the pain is as sharp as we think.

> ⚠️ **Numbers below are illustrative planning assumptions, not projections or
> promises.** Every figure is an input you can change. Market-size anchors are
> cited; the pricing proforma is a model, and models are only as honest as their
> assumptions (that's literally the brand). Verify the starred figures before any
> external use.

---

## 1. One-line thesis

Every retirement planning engine (eMoney, MoneyGuide, RightCapital, Lifeview) is
built for **savers**. Nothing is built for **builders** — and the people who feel
that gap most painfully aren't founders (who are illiquid and won't pay), but the
**advisors, RIAs, and attorneys** who keep bouncing off a *Wall of Indifference*
trying to land those founders as clients *before* the exit turns them into whales.

**Sell the key to that wall. Founder-native model, advisor-native customer.**

---

## 2. The problem — the Wall of Indifference

An advisor walks in to pitch an entrepreneur using a Monte Carlo built for a
schoolteacher: steady W-2 income, linear contributions, a 4%-rule drawdown. The
founder feels nothing. Nothing on the screen resembles their financial life —
lumpy or no salary, illiquid concentrated equity, vesting, 83(b)/QSBS, dilution,
secondary sales, a single binary liquidity event, and the intent to *reinvest the
win into the next venture.* So the founder meets the pitch with indifference, and
a future whale swims away.

Advisors and attorneys **know this wall.** They've bounced off it their whole
careers watching pre-exit founders (who become the most lucrative clients alive
*after* liquidity) stay unadvised because no tool speaks builder.

**The product's job is not "plan for founders." It's: give the advisor the one
artifact that makes an entrepreneur lean in — so they land the relationship years
before the money is liquid.** That's a painkiller (client acquisition), not a
vitamin (another calculator).

---

## 3. ICP — who actually pays

The buyer is **not** the founder. The buyer is whoever is trying to catch the
founder early:

| Segment | Why they buy | Willingness to pay |
|---|---|---|
| **RIAs / financial advisors** courting founders, business owners, equity-rich prospects | Land future-whale clients pre-liquidity; differentiate from every saver-tool advisor | Already pay **$1,400–$3,600/yr** for eMoney/MoneyGuide |
| **Wealth-management teams** at wirehouses / independents with an entrepreneur book | Same, plus a repeatable prospecting motion | High; tooling is overhead, not COGS |
| **Estate, tax & startup attorneys** (QSBS, trusts, 83(b), formation) | Show a founder *why plan now*; win the engagement early | Bill $400–$1,000/hr; a client-winning tool is trivial |

**End user / beneficiary:** the founder (the advisor's prospect). Modeled *for*
them so it resonates; never billed to them.

---

## 4. The product — what it models that saver-tools structurally can't

A probabilistic (Monte Carlo) engine re-founded on the **builder's P&L**:

- **Non-linear / lumpy income** — low or zero salary against tail upside, not a
  steady contribution curve.
- **Illiquid concentrated equity** as the central asset — vesting schedules,
  dilution rounds, secondary windows, single binary liquidity events.
- **Exit-scenario distributions** — not "will I run out?" but "across a range of
  outcomes for *this* cap-table position, what's the distribution of net wealth?"
- **Tax-aware liquidity** — QSBS exclusion, 83(b), ISO/NSO, AMT drag at exercise
  (the levers the equity-comp players already prize).
- **Serial-builder reinvestment** — the founder rolls the win into venture #2; the
  model treats wealth as *redeployed*, not decumulated.
- **"Never outsource" framing** — outputs the operator can drive, not a black box a
  manager hides behind (the brand's spine).

**Duality to hold onto:** *founder-native model, advisor-native buyer.* The advisor
runs it; the founder is the one who finally leans in.

## 7. Business model & proforma pricing

**Model:** per-seat SaaS, billed annually, recurring. Value-priced against the fact
that landing **one** founder who exits is worth years of AUM/fees — so even the top
tier is a rounding error to the buyer.

### Pricing tiers (per seat)

| Tier | Who | Price | Annual |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo** | Individual FA / attorney | $149/mo | **$1,490/yr** (2 mo free annual) |
| **Practice** | 2–10 seats (RIA / law firm) | $119/seat/mo | **$1,190/seat/yr** |
| **Firm** | 11+ seats | $99/seat/mo + onboarding | **$1,188/seat/yr** |
| **Founding cohort** | First 50 firms | locked founder rate | brand-consistent scarcity |

Anchor: eMoney runs **$3,000–$3,600/yr** for a *full platform*; MoneyGuide
**$1,400–$2,100/yr**. A focused client-acquisition wedge priced at **~$1,200–$1,500
/seat/yr** sits credibly below them while doing a job they don't.

### 3-year proforma (illustrative — change the inputs)

**Assumptions:** blended ACV **$1,400/seat/yr**; annual logo churn **18%**; gross
margin **85%** (SaaS; compute/data the main COGS); seat ramp bottoms-up post-
validation.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seats (end of year) | 75 | 300 | 800 |
| Avg seats (in-year) | ~40 | ~185 | ~540 |
| **Exit ARR** | **$105,000** | **$420,000** | **$1,120,000** |
| Recognized revenue | ~$56,000 | ~$259,000 | ~$756,000 |
| Gross profit (85%) | ~$47,600 | ~$220,000 | ~$642,600 |

### Unit economics

- ARPU **$1,400/yr**; gross margin/seat **~$1,190/yr**.
- Avg customer life at 18% churn ≈ **5.5 yrs** → **LTV ≈ $6,500/seat**.
- Target **CAC < $1,190** (≤ 1-yr payback) → **LTV:CAC ≈ 5.5:1.**
- The whole thesis: the buyer's *own* ROI is one landed founder, so CAC should be
  low if the wedge messaging (break the Wall) actually lands.

## 9. Validate-first plan (the loop, applied)

1. **Waitlist the ICP, not founders.** Advisor/RIA/attorney signup on the store,
   pitched as *"break the Wall of Indifference — land founder clients before the
   exit."* Measure who bites. (This is the advisor waitlist tag, now with a thesis.)
2. **Manual delivery.** Tune the existing Monte Carlo spreadsheet to the founder
   profile and hand it to **one real advisor to use in one real entrepreneur
   pitch.** Does it crack the wall? That's the proof and the first case study.
3. **LOIs before code.** A handful of "I'd pay $X/seat/mo for this" from named
   advisors is the green light — and the leverage to build.
4. **Only then productize.** The manual model *is* the software spec.

## 10. Risks & the integrity firewall

- **EquityNav (and incumbents) are funded.** The wedge is *positioning + brand*,
  not raw features — validate that the Wall pain converts before out-building them.
- **No return/performance guarantees.** It's a *modeling* tool; it shows
  distributions of outcomes, never promises them (Rule 2210 line — and a real
  regulatory one for anything advisor-facing).
- **Not advice; the advisor owns every output.** Ships as software the licensed
  professional operates — Brady/the tool doesn't advise the end founder.
- **No fabricated proof.** No invented logos, seat counts, or case studies. The
  first case study is earned in step 2 of the validate plan.
- **Data/security** (cap-table + personal financials) is real scope — flagged for
  the build phase, not the validation phase.
