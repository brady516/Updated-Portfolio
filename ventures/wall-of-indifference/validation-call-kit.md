# Validation call kit — the 300-RIA list

The goal of these calls is **not to sell** (there's no product yet). It's to (1)
confirm the Wall of Indifference is a real, painful, paid problem, (2) gauge
willingness to pay, and (3) recruit a **founding design-partner cohort.** You have
the one thing money can't buy at this stage: a warm list of the exact ICP. Use it
to de-risk the build, not to oversell a thing that doesn't exist.

**Integrity guardrails (say these true things):**
- You're **building** it, not selling it today. If asked "is it done?" → *"No —
  that's why I'm calling you first. I build it with a design-partner cohort so it's
  right."*
- No return/performance promises. It's a **modeling** tool that shows distributions,
  never guarantees outcomes.
- No fabricated proof. Don't invent other firms' names or seat counts. Your first
  case study gets *earned* in the pilot.
- This is a **software** conversation, not securities/advice — clean, but keep it
  there.

---

## Who to call first (segment the 300)

Rank the list before dialing — warm + entrepreneur-heavy converts fastest:

1. **Firms with a founder / business-owner / equity-comp book** (their site, ADV,
   or your memory says they court entrepreneurs). These feel the wall weekly.
2. **Growth-minded independents / breakaways** hunting for a prospecting edge.
3. **Everyone else** — later, once the script is tuned on the first 30 calls.

Call the 20 you know best first — they'll tell you if the script lands and give you
honest, unfiltered pain.

---

## The call (aim 5–8 minutes)

**1. Opener — lead with the scar, as a tested hypothesis (no pitch):**
> "[Name], Brady Gallagher — not selling you anything, promise. Quick reason I
> called: I sat in your seat. Built a book **zero to $22 million in 18 months** on
> 401(k) rollovers — savers — and the tools worked great. Then I went after founder
> money, and **that's where it fell apart.** The same retirement Monte Carlo that
> won me every saver just… bounced off entrepreneurs. I've got a hypothesis about
> why — but I'd rather know: **did you hit that same wall, or was that just me?**"

Then **stop talking.** Let them answer. The "was that just me?" pulls the truth out.

*Why this opener:* the **failure is the hook, not the $22MM.** Success is a
one-sentence ticket of admission — dwell on it and you sound like a stranger
bragging, which raises the wall. The *scar* ("it fell apart on me too") makes you a
peer who lived their problem, and framing it as a **hypothesis you're testing**
turns the whole call into peer discovery instead of a pitch. Keep it to ~25 seconds;
the story is bait to trigger *their* story.

*Integrity:* tell it exactly as it happened — "fell apart" = the saver playbook and
tools didn't translate to builders. The true version is already the strongest one;
don't dramatize it.

**2. Make them feel the Wall (discovery — let them say it):**
> "When you sit across from an entrepreneur or a pre-exit founder and pull up the
> plan — how often does it actually *land*? ... Be honest: does the retirement
> Monte Carlo kind of bounce off them?"

Shut up and listen. You want *them* naming the indifference.

**3. Dig into the pain + current workaround:**
> "When a founder's whole net worth is illiquid equity — no salary, QSBS, a single
> exit event — what do you do today? How do you show them why to engage with you
> *now* instead of after the exit?"

Listen for: "spreadsheets," "I wing it," "I can't really," "we lose them to the
next guy at liquidity."

**4. Quantify the stakes (their ROI, in their words):**
> "Roughly how many entrepreneur or business-owner prospects have you *not* landed
> that you wish you had? ... What's one pre-exit founder worth to you if you catch
> them before liquidity and keep them through it?"

This number is why the tool is cheap. Let them compute it.

**5. The reveal (honest, short):**
> "Here's what I'm building: a Monte Carlo re-founded on a *builder's* P&L —
> illiquid equity, dilution, QSBS, lumpy income, reinvesting the win into the next
> venture. Founder-native model, but it's *your* tool to run in the pitch. The
> whole point is to break that wall and land them early."

**6. The validation ask (the real objective):**
> "Two questions. One — if it did that, is it worth something like **$1,200–$1,500
> a seat a year** to you? ... Two — I'm taking a small **founding cohort of design
> partners**: I build it around your input, you get founder pricing and first
> access. Want one of the seats?"

**7. Close on a concrete next step:**
- **Yes / interested** → "Great — can I put you down for the pilot and send a
  one-pager? First model in your hands to try in a real pitch." (Get the LOI /
  verbal commit.)
- **Maybe** → "Fair. Can I send the one-pager and check back after you've hit the
  wall a couple more times?"
- **No** → "Totally fair — who do you know with a founder-heavy book who'd *hate*
  that wall as much as I think they do?" (Always leave with a referral.)

---

## What counts as a "yes" (track it)

Log every call in a simple sheet — this *is* your demand data:

| Firm | Contact | Feels the wall? (1–5) | WTP signal | Pilot / LOI? | Referral |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Strong signal:** "yes I'd pay ~$X" + agrees to pilot → **design partner.**
- **Soft signal:** feels the wall, won't commit price → nurture with the one-pager.
- **No signal:** doesn't feel the wall → note it. If *many* don't, the thesis is
  wrong and you just saved yourself a build. That's a win too.

Target from the first ~50 calls: **8–15 design partners** = your founding cohort
and your Year-1 proof.

## Voicemail / email (for no-answers)

> **Subject: the wall you hit pitching founders**
>
> [Name] — Brady Gallagher. Not a sales email. I'm building a planning/Monte-Carlo
> tool *for* the founder financial profile (illiquid equity, QSBS, lumpy income)
> that RIAs run to actually land entrepreneur clients before the exit — instead of
> watching the retirement software bounce off them. I'm recruiting a small founding
> cohort of RIAs to build it with. Worth 5 minutes? Reply and I'll send a one-pager.

Keep it that short. The curiosity gap ("the wall you hit") is the hook.

## Objection handling

- **"Is it built yet?"** → *"No — that's exactly why I'm calling you first. I build
  it with the cohort so it's actually right. You get founder pricing for the risk."*
- **"We already use eMoney/MoneyGuide."** → *"Right — for your savers, it's great.
  This is the opposite client: the founder those tools were never built for. It
  doesn't replace your stack; it's what you pull up when the entrepreneur walks in."*
- **"Not interested."** → *"Fair enough — do you work with many founders?"* (If no,
  they're not ICP; move on. If yes, probe the wall once, then get the referral.)
- **"How much again?"** → *"~$1,200–$1,500 a seat a year, founder cohort locks in
  lower. But I'd rather know if it solves the problem before we talk price."*

---

## Why this list changes the plan

Finn cold-emailed strangers to find 25 clients. You're starting with **300 warm
firms of the exact ICP.** That means: near-zero CAC on the founding cohort, LOIs
before a line of code, and — if the wall is real — your first 8–15 design partners
funding the build with committed demand. This is the validate-first loop with the
hardest step already solved. **Make the calls; the calls are the product-market-fit
test.**
