# The 90-second phone close — Go Legit Local

Use when a lead **replies** (cold email or partner referral) and you call them. This
is a *low-pressure* close: at $299 with a money-back guarantee, it's a no-brainer, so
your job is to remove friction, not push. **Talk less than half the call.** Ask, then
shut up. The guarantee closes; you just clear the path.

---

## Before you dial (10 sec)
- Who: name, business, trade.
- Channel: did they reply to cold email, or did a **partner** send them? (Partner =
  warmer — name-drop the partner.)
- Have ready: Stripe link, Calendly activation-call link.

---

## Opener — permission + reason (15 sec)

**Cold-email reply:**
> "Hey [name], it's Brady — you replied about getting [Business] off the gmail
> address. Got two minutes? … Great, I'll keep it quick."

**Partner referral (warmer — use it):**
> "Hey [name], Brady here — [partner] mentioned you might want [Business] on a real
> email instead of the gmail. That sound right? … Good — [partner]'s a good guy.
> Two minutes?"

## One discovery question — make *them* say the pain (15 sec)
> "Real quick — when you send a quote or an invoice right now, what's it come from?
> … Yeah. And ever lost a job to somebody else and weren't totally sure why?"

**Then stop talking.** Let them sit in it. That pause is the sale.

## The pitch — transformation, not task (30 sec)
> "Here's what I do, and it's simple. I get you your own professional address —
> [name]@[business].com — so every quote looks like it came from a real, established
> company instead of a side gig. It lands in the **same inbox you already use**, so
> nothing changes for you. I handle all the technical stuff that keeps it out of spam,
> done in 48 hours. And here's the part nobody tells you: **you own all of it, in your
> name. No monthly fee to me, nobody holding it hostage. When I'm done, you don't need
> me.**"

## Price + guarantee (15 sec)
> "It's **$299, one time — not per month.** Software companies charge ninety bucks a
> month for this; I do it once and it's yours forever. And if it's not sending and
> receiving clean, **you don't pay.** Simple as that."

## The close — assumptive, easy next step (15 sec)
> "So here's what happens next — I text you a link to lock it in, then we grab ten
> minutes where I switch it on with you and you send your first email from the new
> address. Want me to send that over?"

→ **Yes:** text the Stripe link, confirm payment, book the activation call (Calendly).
The runbook takes it from here.

---

## Objection handling (stay calm, the guarantee is your safety net)

**"That's a lot / can I afford it?"**
> "Fair — but one bid it helps you win pays for it ten times over, and it's *once,* not
> a monthly bill. Cheapest thing you'll ever do to look bigger than you are."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Totally. Quick — is it the money, or whether it's worth it?" *(surface the real one)*
> "…The offer holds, and remember you don't pay if it doesn't work — so there's really
> nothing to think over. Worst case you're out nothing."

**"I'll just do it myself."**
> "You can — same way I could hang my own drywall. It's three technical records almost
> everybody gets wrong, and getting them wrong lands you in spam folders. Or it's done
> right in 48 hours while you're on a job. Your call."

**"Not sure I even need it."**
> "No pressure. Just — next time you're bidding and it's close, that gmail is quietly
> working against you. It's cheap insurance against looking smaller than you are."

**"Gotta run it by my wife / partner."**
> "Makes sense. It's $299 one-time, done in 48 hours, money back if it doesn't work —
> that's usually the whole conversation. Want me to text you the details so you've got
> them in front of you?"

**"I already have a website guy."**
> "Perfect — this doesn't replace him. If he put you on your own domain email, you're
> set and I'll tell you so in 30 seconds. If you're still on gmail, he didn't finish
> the job. What's your email again?"

## After the yes (don't hang up yet — the two lines that pay)
- **Crew upsell:** "You got techs or an office manager? Matching addresses are $199
  each — makes every estimate look like one company."
- **Referral ask:** "And if you know another [trade] still on a gmail, send 'em my way
  — you're now the guy with the pro email."
- Then: Stripe link sent → activation call booked → runbook.

## Voicemail (no answer)
> "Hey [name], Brady here — you reached out about getting [Business] off the gmail.
> Quick and painless, done in 48 hours, flat fee, and you don't pay if it doesn't work.
> Call or text me back at [number] and I'll get you looking legit. Thanks."

---

## Tone rules
1. **Talk less than half.** Every question, then silence.
2. **It's a no-brainer, not a hard sell** — friction removal, not pressure. Pressure
   reads as the exact "getting taken advantage of" they fear.
3. **Always land "when I'm done, you don't need me."** It's the line no agency will say
   and the reason they trust you.
4. **Let the guarantee do the closing.** "You don't pay if it doesn't work" ends most
   hesitation on its own.
5. **Match their pace** — trades respect direct and brief. Don't fill silence; use it.
