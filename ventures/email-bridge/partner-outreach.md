# Partner recruitment program — courting referral partners

Referral partners are the efficient channel (`funnel-model.md`): each *active*
partner ≈ 0.24 sales/day, so ~8 of them carry your 2/day nut with none of the
cold-email grind. This is the program to find and sign them.

**Finding them = the tool you already have.** Run `leadfinder.py` with partner
queries and *don't* use `--leads-only` (see `partners.sh`). The new work is the
**pitch** — because you're not selling a partner anything. You're offering them
passive money, client goodwill, and zero work. That's a far easier yes than a $299
setup, which is exactly why this channel converts.

---

## Who the partners are (ranked by fit)

1. **Bookkeepers & accountants** — the #1 partner. They see the `gmail` on every
   invoice and receipt they process for their trade clients, they're the trusted
   advisor, and they talk to those clients monthly. Highest trust, highest recurring
   exposure.
2. **Local web designers / freelancers** — they already sell adjacent services and
   they *hate* the email/deliverability part. You take a headache off their plate and
   pay them for it. Natural fit, zero conflict.
3. **Business-formation / LLC-filing services, small-biz consultants** — they onboard
   brand-new businesses at the exact moment "you need a real email" is obvious.
4. **Trade suppliers, chambers of commerce, BNI/networking groups** — one warm intro
   to a room of trades. Slower, but high-leverage.

(Adjacent trades referring each other is real too, but that's *customer* referral —
handled at delivery, not here.)

---

## Why they say yes (the value prop is to THEM)

- **$50 per closed referral** — passive money for forwarding a name.
- **They look good to their client** — solved a problem, at no cost to the client.
- **Zero work** — they forward your link or a name; you do everything.
- **No conflict** — you don't compete with them; you do the tech thing they don't want.
- **(Web designers especially):** you handle the annoying deliverability records they
  dread. You make them look complete without the hassle.

You're handing them money and goodwill. Lead with *their* upside, never with your
product.

---

## The outreach (a relationship sale, not a blast)

Partners are low-volume, high-value — treat each like a person, not a list.

**Email 1 — the offer (short):**

> **Subject:** $50 per client, zero work on your end
>
> Hi [name] — I set local tradespeople up with professional email (getting them off
> `bobsdrywall1987@gmail.com` onto `bob@bobsdrywall.com`). You work with exactly those
> businesses. If you ever run into one embarrassed by their gmail, send them my way —
> **I pay you $50 for every one that signs up**, they get a flat-fee setup done for
> them, and you look like the person who solved it. No work on your end, no cost to
> them to hear about it. Worth a 10-minute call to swap details?
> — [you] · [physical address] · unsubscribe: [link]

**The call / coffee — the real close.** Local partners sign over a 15-minute call or
coffee, not email. Confirm the $50, hand them collateral to forward, and get their
*first* referral teed up before you hang up.

**Follow-up:** give them a forward-ready blurb + your link/cards so referring is
one click. Friction kills partner channels.

---

## Kickback mechanics & terms

- **$50 per referral that closes and pays.** Consider **$75 after their 5th** to keep
  active partners active.
- **Attribution:** a `?ref=` code on the landing form, or "who sent you?" on intake, or
  a unique link per partner. Keep it dead simple.
- **Pay fast** — Venmo/Zelle/check within 48h of the client paying. Fast payment is the
  single biggest driver of *repeat* referrals.
- **Light paperwork:** a one-page partner agreement — commission, non-exclusive,
  independent (they're not your employee), no guarantees, either side can stop anytime.
- **Tax rigor (the operator move):** track partner payouts; anyone you pay **$600+/yr
  gets a 1099-NEC.** Clean books here also protect your exit multiple.
- **No regulatory snag:** paying referral fees for a *service* is normal and legal
  (this isn't securities — no licensing issue). Just disclose it honestly; never let a
  partner make claims you can't back.

## Activation & retention (where the real work is)

A signed partner who never refers is dead weight. The program lives or dies on
*activation*:

- **Get their first referral fast**, pay it instantly, and make a big deal of it — the
  first $50 is what makes the channel real to them.
- **Monthly 2-minute check-in:** share a win, restock their cards, stay top of mind.
- **Track the number that matters — ACTIVE partners** (referred in the last 30 days),
  not signed partners.

| Partner | Type | Signed | Referrals (30d) | Active? | Paid to date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Sourcing (reuse the finder)

`leadfinder.py` finds partners the same way it finds trades — different query, no
freemail filter. See `partners.sh` for ready-to-run commands. Ignore the `status`
column on partner runs (freemail vs. pro is irrelevant here); you want *all* their
contact emails.

---

## Why this is the moat (and the exit)

Cold email is rented attention; a **partner network is owned distribution.** Partners
refer other partners, they're near-zero marginal cost, and they compound. A buyer pays
*up* for a business with an active, documented referral network — it's the difference
between selling a lead-list and selling a *channel.* Build 8 active partners and you've
built both your 2/day engine and a chunk of your exit multiple.
