# Rules of Engagement — Go Legit Local

The terms of the client relationship. **These aren't fine print — they're part of the
offer.** The shady operators run on hidden fees, lock-in, and surprise charges; clear,
fair terms set *up front* are the anti-Scourge in operational form, and they're what
keeps a $299 job from quietly becoming unpaid lifetime IT support.

Guiding rule: **no surprises, ever.** Every charge is disclosed and authorized before it
happens. That single promise is the whole brand.

---

## 1. Payment terms (by tier)

- **Tier 1 — Email ($299):** paid **in full, up front**, before any provisioning. It's
  small and fast; a deposit adds friction to a no-brainer. Payment before work, always.
- **Tier 2 / 3 — Presence & Full Package (four figures):** **50% deposit to start,
  50% on delivery.** The deposit protects your cashflow, filters tire-kickers, and is
  standard for any build. No work begins until the deposit clears.
- **Care plan (MRR):** billed monthly, cancel anytime (stops the next renewal).

## 2. Card on file

- **Required for Tier 2/3 and Care-plan clients** — it enables the balance payment,
  future break-fix, and the monthly plan without chasing invoices.
- **Optional for the $299 email** — just charge it in full; don't add a vault step to a
  fast yes unless they're likely to upgrade.
- **The rule that makes it honest:** a card on file is a *convenience, not a license.*
  **You never charge it without disclosing and getting a yes first.** No surprise
  charges — that's the exact "taken advantage of" you exist to kill. Stripe makes
  saved-card + authorized one-off charges clean; use it that way.

## 3. Scope & the support boundary

What's included is **the setup, done right, and fixes to *what I built* for a defined
window.** What's *not* included is ongoing IT support for their whole digital life. The
boundary is the difference between a business and a swamp.

- **Included:** everything in the tier + a **30-day fix window** on anything *I* set up
  that isn't working right. That's on me.
- **Not included:** their computer/printer/phone, unrelated software, new devices,
  third-party outages, teaching sessions beyond the handoff, or things they (or someone
  else) changed that broke it. Those are billable or a Care-plan matter.
- **The paths after the window:** **break-fix at $[75]/incident** (or $[95]/hr), or the
  **Care plan at $[25–99]/mo** for updates, monitoring, and priority fixes.

## 4. The tech-support paragraph (paste-ready, client-facing)

> **How support works — plain and simple.** For **30 days** after your setup, if
> anything I built for you isn't working right, I'll fix it at no charge — that part's
> on me. After that, or for anything outside what I set up (your computer, a printer, a
> new device, or changes you or someone else made), I'm glad to help at a flat
> **$[75] per fix** — just reach out and I'll tell you the cost before I touch anything.
> If you'd rather never think about it, I offer a simple **Care plan for $[25]/month**
> that covers updates, monitoring, and priority fixes. Either way, **you own everything
> and you're never locked in.** I'd rather set clear expectations now than surprise you
> later — that's the whole point of working with me.

## 5. Internal policy (for you / a VA)

- **Log every request** against the client's tier and window. In-window + my-build =
  free fix. Anything else = quote first, then bill.
- **Quote before touching.** Never do "quick" unpaid work "just this once" — that's how
  the boundary erodes. A 2-minute favor teaches them the next one is free too.
- **Route ongoing needs to the Care plan.** A client asking for repeat help isn't a
  nuisance — it's an MRR upsell (which also lifts the exit multiple).
- **Everything stays in the client's name.** No holding domains/logins hostage, ever —
  the boundary protects *you*, it never traps *them.*

## 6. Ties to the written terms

The refund/cancellation, license, and disclaimer language lives in the site's
`terms.html` (48-hour deliverable guarantee, own-it license, no lock-in). Rules of
Engagement is the *operating* policy; terms.html is the *legal* page. Keep them
consistent.
